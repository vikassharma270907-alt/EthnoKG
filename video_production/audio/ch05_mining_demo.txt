Next, we move to the Literature Mining Engine. This is where raw scientific literature is transformed into structured, queryable knowledge. Researchers can choose to upload local PDF files, input an open-access paper URL, paste raw ethnobotanical field notes, or select from our curated benchmark collection of landmark ethnopharmacology publications. For this demonstration, we select the historic Nobel Prize-winning paper by Tu Youyou documenting the discovery of artemisinin from Artemisia annua for the treatment of intermittent fevers and malaria. When we trigger the 12-Stage Literature Mining Pipeline, the engine executes asynchronously. Notice the animated stage progress visualizer dynamically illuminating each step: document cleaning, paragraph splitting, taxonomic Kew MPNS verification, MeSH condition alignment, preparation extraction, and provenance binding. In seconds, the pipeline outputs structured extraction cards. Crucially, look at the verbatim evidence preview beneath each triple: the exact historical passage from Ge Hong's ancient handbook and modern clinical confirmations are displayed side-by-side with high-precision confidence gauges, guaranteeing total auditability.

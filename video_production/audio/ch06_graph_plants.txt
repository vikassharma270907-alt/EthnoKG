Now, let us explore our flagship visualization: the Interactive Knowledge Graph. Powered by Cytoscape dot J S, this dynamic network canvas maps multidimensional ethnopharmacological relationships with physics-based layout controls. Green nodes represent verified botanical taxa; amber nodes represent medical conditions and symptoms; cyan nodes highlight bioactive phytochemical compounds; and purple nodes represent cultural medical traditions. Users can effortlessly pan, zoom, toggle between force-directed CoSE, concentric, and breadthfirst layouts, and filter nodes by evidence tier or confidence threshold. Clicking on any edge instantly opens the Evidence Provenance Drawer on the right. Here, researchers can read the exact supporting verbatim excerpt, view the publication year, authors, and DOI link, and inspect the preparation details. Next, we transition to the Plant Intelligence Explorer. Searching for plants like Withania somnifera, commonly known as Ashwagandha, or Curcuma longa, opens an authoritative Plant Intelligence Dossier. The dossier displays Kew MPNS accepted binomial names, verified vernacular synonyms across regional dialects, plant organs utilized, traditional preparation methods, linked therapeutic conditions, and primary literature citations.
